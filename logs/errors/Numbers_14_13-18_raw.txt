      "application": "Understanding verse 18 teaches us what genuine repentance means. We cannot expect God to overlook sin as though it never happened ('venaqeh lo yenaqqeh')—but neither should we despair of His willingness to forgive when we turn to Him. The passage teaches us that God is:  (1) Patient with our weakness and progress (erekh appayim), (2) Fundamentally committed to relationship with us (rav-chesed), (3) Willing to bear the burden of our sin if we confess it (nosei avon vafesha), yet (4) Insistent that sin has real consequences we must reckon with ('venaqeh lo yenaqqeh'). When we intercede for others—for family members struggling with sin, for friends in rebellion, for whole communities in need of redemption—we can quote God's own character back to Him as Moses does. We can say: 'God, You have declared Yourself to be merciful and patient. I ask that you show that mercy to this person I love, not because they deserve it but because You have promised to extend it to those who turn to You.' The passage also teaches us about generational responsibility: our faithfulness or unfaithfulness affects those who come after us. We are called to break cycles of sin and establish patterns of righteousness, trusting that God's patience extends to our children and that they can find redemption even if we have failed. This is why family home evening, family prayer, and covenant-keeping are so crucial in Latter-day Saint life—they establish patterns that ripple forward in blessing across generations."

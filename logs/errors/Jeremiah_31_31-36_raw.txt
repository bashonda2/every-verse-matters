Individual members can also draw comfort from this verse: personal covenants made in the temple are not contingent on circumstances or feelings but are grounded in something as permanent and reliable as the cosmos itself. When one's faith is tested or when promises seem delayed, this verse invites reflection on God's track record: if God can sustain the sun in its course for millennia, if God can preserve a people through exile and diaspora, surely God will fulfill promises made to faithful individuals. The verse also invites humility: human kingdoms rise and fall, but God's purposes endure. This should prevent both triumphalism (assuming our current circumstances will never change) and despair (assuming God cannot overcome current obstacles).

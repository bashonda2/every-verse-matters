Just as Abraham's covenant required that every generation circumcise the next generation's males, parents in the Church are stewards of covenant identity for their children. Baptism and temple covenants must be actively perpetuated; they cannot be assumed or passively inherited. Parents are responsible for teaching and enabling their children to understand, enter, and maintain their own covenants. Second, *inclusive covenant membership*. The principle that purchased servants and foreigners could become full covenant members teaches that the covenant community is not exclusive to 'insiders' by birth or status. The modern Church emphasizes this principle: anyone, regardless of background, can join the covenant people through baptism and membership. Conversion—the acceptance of covenants—makes a person fully part of God's people. This challenges modern members to resist tribalism, cultural chauvinism, or assumptions that 'real' covenant members are only those born into the Church.

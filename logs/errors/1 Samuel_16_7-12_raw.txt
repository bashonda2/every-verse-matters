Most significantly, both are described in terms of their hearts: David as a man after God's own heart, and Christ as the one who came to do the Father's will perfectly. The anointing of David is the foundational type of Christ's anointing as King and High Priest.",
    "application": "This verse contains several profound applications. First, the physical description of David—that he is genuinely attractive and handsome—combined with God's statement that he does not judge by appearance, teaches that being physically attractive or impressive is not wrong or disqualifying. Rather, it is secondary to the condition of the heart. You may be blessed with attractiveness, talent, intelligence, or other impressive qualities. These are gifts from God. But they are not what make you chosen or valuable in God's sight. Use such gifts in service, but do not mistake them for your worth. Second, the absolute brevity of God's identification ('This is he') invites you into the experience of being chosen. God's election is not the result of a lengthy deliberation where you must prove yourself. It is not a conditional offer waiting for you to meet additional criteria. It is a declaration of chosenness based on what God knows and sees. This is the foundation of grace: you are chosen not because you have convinced God of your worthiness, but because God has chosen you. Finally, the anointing of David marks him publicly as set apart. In your own life, are you living as though you are anointed—set apart for God's purposes? The teaching invites you to understand that if you have made covenants with God (in baptism, in the temple, in priesthood ordination), you have been marked as set apart. You are not your own; you belong to God. This does not mean you immediately have power or position, but it means you are claimed by God and destined for his purposes. Live accordingly."

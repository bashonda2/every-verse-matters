The propitiation (covering, atonement) is Christ's blood, applied to God's justice for the remission of sin. Leviticus 4:30 is the shadow of this substance.",
    "application": "Leviticus 4:30 teaches that atonement is not achieved through the commoner's private intention or the commoner's feeling sorry, but through a public, priestly act that applies the blood—the symbol of life itself—to the altar where God's presence is located. In modern terms, this means that repentance is not complete until it is processed through the priesthood and formal ordinances. A person may feel genuine remorse privately, but the restoration of covenant standing requires:  (1) confession to a priesthood leader (the equivalent of the priest receiving the blood), (2) the formal reception of forgiveness through priesthood authority and ordinance (the equivalent of the blood being applied), and (3) the person's covenant that they will not repeat the sin. The priest's action—taking the blood on his finger and applying it to the horns and base—is not a formality but a necessary mediation. The blood must be applied; remorse alone is insufficient. For members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, this principle applies to the repentance process: those who have sinned seriously should not be content with private prayer and personal resolve. They should seek the priesthood leader's guidance and participate in the formal covenant processes (including excommunication and rebaptism if necessary) to ensure that the transaction is complete and public. The commoner cannot apply the blood themselves; neither can a sinner apply the blood of Christ to themselves alone. The priesthood, the ordinances, and the community are all necessary parts of the atonement process."

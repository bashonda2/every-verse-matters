First, it affirms the blessing of seeing family relationships flourish across generations—the joy of being a grandparent or great-grandparent, the privilege of watching children grow in the gospel and teach their own children. If you have had this blessing, it is evidence of YHWH's favor; gratitude is the proper response. Second, for those who have not experienced this blessing (due to infertility, childlessness, loss, estrangement, or early death in the family), the verse invites trust in God's expanded definition of family. Through temple covenants and the restoration of all things, the faithful will witness the multiplication and continuation of their family lines in the celestial kingdom. The plea for 'peace upon Israel' at the psalm's close universalizes the blessing: you are praying not just for your own family's peace but for the whole covenant community to dwell in peace and righteousness. This elevates individual blessing to a national and ultimately eternal scale. In modern contexts where war, violence, and instability threaten families, this closing prayer is urgent: Shalom al Yisrael—peace on Israel, on the Church, on all the faithful. The individual who fears the LORD and walks in his ways becomes part of the prayer-force that calls peace down upon the whole covenant community."

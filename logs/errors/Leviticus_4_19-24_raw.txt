When we lay hands on the sacrament bread and cup, we perform a gesture paralleling the ancient hand-laying on the sacrifice—we identify ourselves with Christ, we establish Him as our representative before God, and we confirm that His atonement becomes the means of our cleansing. This gesture is not ceremonial decoration but the foundation of the covenant moment. Further, the slaughter at the 'place where the burnt offering is slaughtered' teaches that we cannot separate our confession of sin (the sin offering) from our commitment to devotion (the burnt offering). To truly repent and be forgiven, we must simultaneously commit to living devoted lives. The two move together. When we discover we have sinned (as the nasi discovers his transgression, v. 23), we do not merely confess and seek forgiveness; we lay our hands on the covenant (through sacrament, through temple, through recommitment), identify ourselves with Christ's perfect offering, and dedicate ourselves to his service. The ancient nasi's goat dies so the nasi can live; Christ dies so we can live. Our participation in the covenant system (sacrament, temple, priesthood ordinations) continues the gesture of hand-laying and the principle of substitution into the modern dispensation. We are forgiven not because we deserve it but because Christ, our perfect substitute, has paid the price."
